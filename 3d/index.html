Model notes
  • Machine presets: "Standard bottom stirrer" is a generic, illustrative class of bottom-mounted linear induction stirrer driven at 0.2–1 Hz through a 500 mm air gap of refractory and plate. The values are rounded and are not product specifications. "Compact demo" is the original 2 m toy. The stirrer drawing is illustrative, after the general layout of this machine class (steel tray, stacked pancake coils in slots between laminated core blocks, clamp bars, end walls), not a product drawing. The Plant button adds a generic furnace shell and supports, an inverter cabinet, a control and HMI cabinet, a cooling unit, mains disconnect, junction box, RTD box and blower, with the cable runs between them. Power cables are drawn as three pairs (one per phase), with pulses that follow the current. Layout and sizes are illustrative.
  • Force: a traveling-wave push in the melt above the stirrer. It decays with height as exp(−2·a·d), and the gap adds a factor exp(−2·a·g) before the melt. The decay rate a comes from the skin depth δ = √(1 / (π f μ₀ σ)) with σ = 3.8 MS/m for liquid aluminum (approximate). A melt that is already moving sees a lower slip frequency, which acts like a deeper skin depth: δ/√slip. The optional Field model adds the pole-pitch limit: α² = (π/τ)² + 2i·slip/δ², a = Re α. Current scales force as I².
  • Thrust is normalised so that the preset gap, frequency and pole pitch give the nominal force. Raising frequency or widening the gap then reduces the force that reaches the melt, and the readout shows it as a percentage of that design value.
  • Flow: 3D incompressible solver (stable fluids, MacCormack dye advection) on a coarse grid (Fast) or about 1.6 times finer (Fine). Walls and floor are no-slip and the top is free-slip. Vorticity confinement and weak drag stand in for turbulence. Sim time is compressed so that 5–10 minute reversal cycles can be watched.
  • Cross flow is the fastest motion across the bath width. It comes from the stirrer being narrower than the bath and from friction on the side walls.
  • Left out: free-surface motion, thermal convection, heating of the window plate, side-mounted installations and any calibration against a real furnace. Qualitative only. Do not use it to size or predict a real installation.
